## RUB Segment
### General Trends & Key Metrics (RUB)
- **Revenue and Order Dynamics:** Sustained growth in key performance indicators was observed throughout the analyzed period. By the end of the period, revenue increased by nearly RUB 2M, while weekly order volume expanded almost threefold — rising from ~1,000 to ~2,700 orders.

- **Average Revenue per Order (AOV):** Maintained a stable baseline level, averaging approximately RUB 500. However, the metric demonstrates high volatility with distinct localized spikes and drops, driven by individual marketing campaigns and sales launches for major events.

- **Seasonality:** A structural shift emerged starting in the first decade of September: weekly revenue consistently surpassed RUB 1M, and order volume exceeded 2,000 units. This growth was propelled by the end of the vacation season and the launch of the new performing arts season (marked by a sharp surge in the "Theater" category). Additionally, mid-September marked the initial surge in demand for New Year's holiday shows.

- **Intra-Week Dynamics:** User behavior follows a pronounced weekly pattern, characterized by peak activity toward the end of the week (especially on Fridays) and a slowdown early in the week.

### Sales Structure: Categories, Devices, and Geography
#### Categories and Platforms (Devices)
- **Concert Dominance:** Concerts serve as the primary product growth driver, generating 56% of total revenue.

- **Mobile-First Audience:** 79% of all orders are placed via mobile devices, while desktop accounts for 20%. Other devices combined account for less than 0.5%.

- **Behavioral Differences (Desktop vs. Mobile):**

-- **Average Revenue per Order:** Desktop users exhibit a slightly higher AOV — RUB 561 vs. RUB 544 on mobile devices.

-- **Category Preferences:** Mobile users show a higher preference for concerts (58% vs. 51% on desktop). On desktop, users buy theater tickets more frequently (28% vs. 22% on mobile). The top event on mobile is a concert, whereas on desktop, it is a theatrical performance.

#### Geographic Concentration
Demand is heavily concentrated. Among the top 5 regions, the absolute leaders in revenue and order volume are:

- Kamenevsky Region: RUB 61,555,900

- Severoyarsk Region: RUB 25,453,228

### Analysis of Events, Venues, and Anomalies
#### Pricing Models and Event Strategies
Two distinct, opposing go-to-market strategies stand out across the market:

- **Mass Market Segment (Revenue & Volume Leader):** Event `9cc55c15-4375-4129-9979-3129688ba1b4` (Concert) ranks 1st in total revenue and ticket sales volume, but ranks near the bottom in average ticket price.

- **Premium Segment (High AOV):** Event `b443dc5a-fff2-4b69-8684-0719fff49b59` (Concert) ranks 2nd in revenue despite lower sales volume, driven by the highest average ticket price.

A similar inverse relationship ("volume vs. price") is observed at the venue level: Lestnica Design Studio Limited leads in order volume with a low average check, whereas Spelka Science Museum Incorporated generates a high average check with lower sales volume.

#### Localized Anomalies and Spikes
- **August 3:** An isolated spike in AOV was recorded, tied to the top event where multiple tickets were purchased within single orders (3,958 orders accounted for 10,241 tickets sold).

- **September 3 & October 1:** Distinct spikes in order volume occurred alongside a simultaneous drop in average check size. Analysis indicates this anomaly was driven by a surge in the "Sports" category (likely reflecting initial sales launches for high-demand marquee matches at affordable prices).

## KZT Segment
### General Trends & Key Metrics (KZT)
- **Revenue and Order Dynamics:** Revenue remained relatively flat at low levels (under KZT 200,000) throughout most of the observed period. Two major spikes occurred: June 3–5 (revenue surged above KZT 1M) and June 11 (revenue exceeded KZT 11M).

- **Average Revenue per Order (AOV):** Fluctuated around KZT 10,000, with a period-wide mean of KZT 4,995.28. Major peaks occurred on June 11–14 and July 24, exceeding KZT 10,000 due to high-demand surges for specific events.

- **Seasonality:** No distinct seasonal shifts or weekly recurring patterns were identified in the aggregate dataset. However, a noticeable uptick in user interest for the "Other" category occurred starting mid-August.

### Sales Structure: Categories, Devices, and Geography
#### Categories and Platforms (Devices)
- **Concert Dominance:** Concerts account for nearly 95% of total revenue, acting as the core revenue driver.

- **"Other" Category Surge:** From the second decade of August through the end of the observed period, there was a sustained increase in audience interest for events categorized under "Other."

- **Mobile-First Audience:** 64% of all orders are placed via mobile devices, while desktop accounts for nearly 36%. Other devices combined account for less than 0.5%.

- **Behavioral Differences (Desktop vs. Mobile):**

-- **Average Revenue per Order:** Desktop users exhibit a higher AOV — KZT 6,418.46 vs. KZT 4,446.19 on mobile.

-- **Category Preferences:** Desktop shows a higher share of concert sales (97% vs. 93% on mobile). On mobile devices, users purchase sports event tickets more frequently (1.6% vs. 0.6% on desktop). The top 5 events are identical across both device types.

#### Geographic Concentration
Demand is highly concentrated. Out of four available regions, two dominate the landscape: the Shanyr Region and the Verkhorechnaya Region generate virtually all revenue, account for the majority of orders, and show an average revenue per ticket nearly three times higher than the remaining two regions.

### Analysis of Events, Venues, and Anomalies
- The top event `76af4f88-c554-4e9e-9df4-1cc8aeec8959` (Concert) drove the revenue and order volume peak on June 11, while the #2 event `0686ac7b-6f3e-4177-bb73-16b54acd1223` (Concert) accounted for the June 3–5 peak. The top event also achieved the highest average revenue per ticket.

- One event from the "Other" category entered the top 5 by total revenue. Tickets for this event sold steadily over a one-month window (August–September), creating a smooth distribution without major revenue spikes and resulting in only minor peaks in order volume.

## Product Team Recommendations
### Managing Seasonality and Intra-Week Dynamics
- **Marketing Shift (August – September):** Launch presales for theatrical seasons and New Year's holiday shows in the first half of August. This will smooth out the summer lull and capture audience demand ahead of the September demand surge.

- **Promotional Calendar Timing (Wednesday – Thursday):** Given the strong Friday peak pattern, schedule main marketing pushes, sales announcements, and email campaigns for mid-week (Wednesday–Thursday) to build momentum leading into the weekend.

### Event Portfolio & Pricing Strategy
- **Protect and Scale the Concert Category:** As the core revenue generator (56%), concerts require continuous catalog maintenance. Maintain a balanced portfolio between high-volume mass events (driving volume) and premium niche events (driving high order value).

- **Develop the Theater Category:** Since theater attracts users with a higher average check (especially on desktop), this category warrants cross-selling support (e.g., recommending plays to concert ticket buyers).

### Revenue Optimization
- **Bundled Offers for Sports Events:** For high-demand, low-AOV sports releases (such as those on September 3 and October 1), introduce upsell bundles ("Ticket + Merchandise / Parking / Concessions") to increase overall per-order revenue.

- **Staggered Sales Launch Management:** Stagger the launch dates of high-priced, high-demand events away from high-volume sports releases to prevent diluting user attention and maintain a steady baseline AOV without sharp dips.
